### Exercise 14. A Small Change in the Right-Hand Side

Compare the systems

$$
\begin{cases}
x+y=2, \\
2x-y=1, \\
3x=3
\end{cases}
$$

and

$$
\begin{cases}
x+y=2, \\
2x-y=1, \\
3x=3.1.
\end{cases}
$$

Check the consistency of each system and explain why a small change in one number can change the type of the system.

> **Why this exercise:** shows the difference between a consistent and an inconsistent system without introducing new theory.

**Solution:**

For the first system:
Equations 1 and 2 ($x+y=2$ and $2x-y=1$) sum to $3x=3 \implies x=1$, giving $y=1$. The third equation $3x=3$ agrees with $x=1$. The system is consistent with solution $(1,1)$.

For the second system:
The first two equations again dictate $x=1$ and $y=1$. However, the third equation requires $3x=3.1 \implies x=1.033$. This is contradictory. The system is inconsistent.

A small change shifted the third line so it no longer passes perfectly through the intersection point of the other two lines.
