### Exercise 2. A Line in Space

Given

$$
L:(x,y,z)=(2,-1,3)+t(1,2,-2).
$$

State a point on the line and its direction vector, check whether $P=(4,3,-1)$ lies on $L$, and find the point corresponding to $t=-1$.

> **Why this exercise:** reinforces the interpretation of parametric form as “point + direction.”

**Answer 2**

From the parametric equation, a point on the line is $(2,-1,3)$ and the direction vector is $(1,2,-2)$.

To check if $P=(4,3,-1)$ lies on $L$, we set the coordinates equal to the parametric equations:

$$
\begin{cases}
2+t = 4, \\
-1+2t = 3, \\
3-2t = -1.
\end{cases}
$$

Solving the first equation yields $t=2$. Substituting $t=2$ into the other equations gives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-1+2(2) &= 3 \\
3-2(2) &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

Since all equations are satisfied by a single value of $t$, the point $P$ lies on the line.

To find the point corresponding to $t=-1$, substitute this value into the line equation:

$$
(x,y,z) = (2-1, -1-2, 3-(-2)) = (1, -3, 5)
$$
