### Exercise 3. Plane Through a Point with a Given Normal Vector

Find the equation of the plane through $P=(1,-2,3)$ and perpendicular to $n=(2,1,-1)$. Check the result by substituting the point $P$ and explain the role of the normal vector.

> **Why this exercise:** shows the basic construction of a plane from a point and a normal vector.

**Answer 3**

The general equation of a plane with normal vector $n=(A,B,C)$ passing through a point $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ is:

$$
A(x-x_0) + B(y-y_0) + C(z-z_0) = 0
$$

Substituting the given values:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(x-1) + 1(y-(-2)) - 1(z-3) &= 0 \\
2x - 2 + y + 2 - z + 3 &= 0 \\
2x + y - z + 3 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Verification for point $P(1,-2,3)$:

$$
2(1) + (-2) - (3) + 3 = 0
$$

**Explanation:** The normal vector defines the orthogonal orientation of the plane in space. Any vector lying entirely within the plane (formed by connecting $P$ to any other point $X$ on the plane) must be perpendicular to the normal vector, which forces their dot product to be zero.
