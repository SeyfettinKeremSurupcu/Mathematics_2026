### Exercise 9. Distances

Compute:
1. the distance from $P=(2,3)$ to the line $3x+4y-10=0$,
2. the distance from $Q=(2,-1,3)$ to the plane $2x-2y+z-4=0$.

Compare the two formulas and explain their common connection with the normal direction.

> **Why this exercise:** organizes two similar formulas through one geometric idea.

**Answer 9**

Distance from $P$ to the line:

$$
d_1 = \frac{|3(2) + 4(3) - 10|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}} = \frac{|6 + 12 - 10|}{5} = \frac{8}{5}
$$

Distance from $Q$ to the plane:

$$
d_2 = \frac{|2(2) - 2(-1) + 1(3) - 4|}{\sqrt{2^2+(-2)^2+1^2}} = \frac{|4 + 2 + 3 - 4|}{3} = \frac{5}{3}
$$

**Comparison and Explanation:** Both distance formulas evaluate the respective general equation at the given point and divide by the length of the normal vector. Geometrically, both formulas operate by taking a displacement vector from the geometric object (line or plane) to the target point, and calculating its scalar projection exactly along the normal direction.
