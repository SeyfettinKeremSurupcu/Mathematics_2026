### Exercise 5. Hyperbola and Asymptotes

For

$$\frac{x^2}{16}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1,$$

find the intercepts with the $x$-axis, the asymptotes, and make a sketch. Explain why the asymptotes describe the behavior of the curve far from the center.

#### Solution 5

Here $a = 4$ and $b = 3$.

- **$x$-intercepts ($y=0$):** $(-4,0)$ and $(4,0)$.
- **Asymptotes:**

$$y = \pm \frac{3}{4}x$$

- **Behavior far from the center:** Solving for $y$ gives

$$y = \pm \frac{3}{4}x\sqrt{1 - \frac{16}{x^2}}$$

As $\vert{}x\vert{} \to \infty$, $\frac{16}{x^2} \to 0$ and $\sqrt{1 - \frac{16}{x^2}} \to 1$. The distance between the curve and the lines $y = \pm \frac{3}{4}x$ tends to $0$, so the asymptotes approximate the branches at large distances from the origin.

```text
          y
          ^
      \   |   /   y = (3/4)x
       \  |  /
     )  \ | /  (
    -4---\|/---4---> x
     )  / | \  (
       /  |  \
      /   |   \   y = -(3/4)x
```
