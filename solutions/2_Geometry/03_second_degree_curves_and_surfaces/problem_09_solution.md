### Exercise 9. Ellipsoid and Its Cross-Sections

Consider

$$\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}+\frac{z^2}{16}=1.$$

Give the semiaxis lengths, describe the cross-section $z=0$, and explain how the cross-sections $z=c$ change for $\vert{}c\vert{}<4$.

#### Solution 9

- **Semiaxis lengths:** $3$ along $x$, $2$ along $y$, and $4$ along $z$.
- **Cross-section $z=0$:**

$$\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1$$

An ellipse in the $xy$-plane centered at $(0,0,0)$ with semiaxes $3$ and $2$.

- **Cross-sections $z=c$ for $\vert{}c\vert{}<4$:**

$$\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4} = 1 - \frac{c^2}{16}$$

Setting $k = \sqrt{1 - \frac{c^2}{16}} > 0$ gives an ellipse with semiaxes $3k$ and $2k$. The ellipse is largest at $c=0$ and shrinks symmetrically as $\vert{}c\vert{} \to 4$, collapsing to the points $(0,0,\pm 4)$ at $c = \pm 4$.
