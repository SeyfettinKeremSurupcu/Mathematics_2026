### Exercise 12. Recognizing a Surface from Cross-Sections

A certain surface has the following properties:
- the cross-section $z=0$ is a point,
- for $z>0$, the cross-sections $z=\mathrm{const}$ are circles with increasing radius,
- vertical cross-sections through the $Oz$ axis are parabolas.

Propose a simple equation for such a surface and justify your choice.

#### Solution 12

A simple equation is the circular paraboloid:

$$z = x^2 + y^2$$

**Justification:**

- For $z=0$: $x^2+y^2=0$ has the unique real solution $(0,0)$, giving the point $(0,0,0)$.
- For $z=c>0$: $x^2+y^2=c$ is a circle of radius $r=\sqrt{c}$, which increases strictly with $c$.
- For any vertical plane through the $Oz$ axis ($x=t\cos\theta$, $y=t\sin\theta$):

$$z = t^2\cos^2\theta + t^2\sin^2\theta = t^2$$

Every vertical cross-section through the $Oz$ axis is a parabola opening upward.
